## Interview Process

### Round 1 – Technical Interview

* **Duration**: ~60 minutes
* **Format**: Technical Discussion

**Questions & Topics Discussed**

* Introduction and overview of professional experience.
* Detailed discussion on work experience so far.
* What work have you done using Rust?
* Current project details and responsibilities.
* Explain ownership and borrowing in Rust with examples.
* What are Smart Pointers? Explanation of `Box`, `Rc`, and `Arc`.
* Trait-based design question:
  * Define a `Shape` trait; implement it for different shape structs; dynamically call a `compute_area` method.
  * Explain why this approach is useful (polymorphism, extensibility).
* Write core logic to reverse a number.
* Error handling in Rust — using `struct` and `enum` with examples.
* Async Rust: `async`/`await`, what is `tokio`, channels in Rust.
* Explain zero-cost abstractions in Rust.
* Explain the interior mutability pattern.
* Explain macros in Rust.
* Discussion on unsafe Rust and when/why it is required.

* **Note**: The interview began on a negative note, as the interviewer repeatedly focused on C/C++ and embedded/automotive experience — which was clearly not on my resume, despite which HR shortlisted me.

## Outcome

* **Result**: Rejected
* **Feedback Received**: Yes — rejection was due to lack of embedded systems and automotive domain experience.
* **Reflections**: The rejection criteria were already visible from my resume and could have been filtered during HR shortlisting. The technical discussion itself was thorough and Rust-focused; time and effort could have been saved with clearer role expectations upfront.

---

## Tips for Future Candidates

* Clarify domain expectations (embedded/automotive vs backend/systems) before the interview.
* Even Rust roles in automotive companies may heavily expect C/C++ and embedded experience.
* Be prepared for domain-driven rejection despite strong technical performance.
* Ensure alignment between HR screening and interviewer expectations before proceeding.
